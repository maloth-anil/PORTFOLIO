I'm a computer engineering student at [Indian Institute of Technology — campus], working across machine learning, computer architecture, and systems security — usually at the point where they overlap.

Most of my time lately goes into audio deepfake detection: reimplementing the SafeEar detection system from scratch in PyTorch, then rebuilding it again with PyTorch Lightning and Hydra to test architectural changes around its Bi-LSTM bottleneck. Alongside that, I've been comparing detection papers from ICASSP, EUSIPCO, and ICCIT to see what different approaches actually catch — and miss.

The rest of my coursework pulls in the same direction: building a shadow stack and control-flow-integrity checker with Intel PIN to catch ROP attacks, writing a compiler optimization visualizer for dead-code elimination and constant propagation, and building a cache simulator to make associativity and replacement policy tangible. Outside class, I spend time on CTF challenges and steganography — most problems, at some scale, come down to finding the signal that was deliberately hidden or faked.
